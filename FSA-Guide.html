Exam day

Format, scoring and a repeatable routine for every case.

What the exam is

  • 2 hours, 13 mini cases, 55 multiple-choice questions, 47 scored exam provider's published format, not in the guide
  • Each case has 3 to 6 questions; the guide suggests 8 to 11 minutes per case. Split screen: case on the left, questions on the right. A calculator and scratch pad are provided.
  • Two question types: single answer (at least four options) and multiple answer (stem says "Choose two/three"; 4 to 6 options). Candidate Handbook
  • Multiple answer is all-or-nothing: miss one correct option, or add one wrong option, and the question scores zero. Handbook: no credit unless all correct answers are selected
  • I could not confirm the handbook's rule for blank or wrong answers. Check it before you sit.

What is and isn't tested

  • Everything needed to answer is in the case. You are not expected to recall industry facts or valuation formulas, or do complex calculations. Simple normalization arithmetic is expected. [Preparing for the Exam]
  • Blue call-out boxes and the Glossary are for conceptual learning and are not testable material. Candidates are not expected to recall specific formulas (e.g. DCF, ROIC, EVA, terminal value).
  • Of the guide's exercises, only Test Your Understanding reflects the exam's style and rigor.
  • The Learning Objective verbs Evaluate / Compare / Differentiate / Assess / Translate / Associate tell you how deeply each topic is tested.

The 6-step routine for every case (about 8 minutes)

  1. Skim the narrative (60 to 90 seconds). Underline what makes the companies differ: revenue streams, inputs, location, regulation, management decisions, any announced change.
  2. Glance at the table. Note which metrics are already percentages, which are absolute, and which are activity metrics (units, facilities, hectares, revenue).
  3. Read the question stem fully. Look for "most", "best supported", "least useful", "not", "both", and whether higher or lower is good.
  4. Answer from the case, not from memory. Every correct option has a sentence or a number in the case behind it. If you cannot point to one, it is a distractor.
  5. Multiple answer: judge each option on its own. Do not hunt for a "matching pair". Count exactly the number asked.
  6. Flag and move. A suggested split: about 104 minutes for the first pass and 16 for flagged items.

How each Learning Objective shows up

LOTypical stemOne-line method
1"How should the topics change given the company's revenue streams or inputs?"Significant stream outside the industry: add that Standard. Stream absent: omit topics. Atypical input: topics get more or less relevant.
2"Which external factor is a risk to A but not B?"Name the component the case actually describes: business, economic, regulatory, location. If the case shows no difference, it is not the answer.
3"Which issue category does this metric capture?" / "Which two issues are interrelated?"Ask what is measured, who is affected, and by what mechanism. Use the neighbour pairs in the 26 issues table.
4"Which N of the five factors…?"Tick only factors with evidence in the case. The guide itself sets factors aside.
5"What should X be normalized by?" / "After normalizing, who improved?"Denominator = what drives the numerator. Percentages are already normalized. Compute both years.
6"Assuming normal distribution… higher than what % of peers?" / "How is it distributed?"z = (value − mean) ÷ SD. Check whether high is good. Mean above median = positive skew.
7"What evidence supports this conclusion?" / "What can you conclude?"Add context: location, regulation, market position, management decision. Missing data is not good performance.
8"Risk or opportunity?" / "Acute or progressive?" / "Likelihood?"Higher = better → opportunity. Single event → acute. Recurring or operating cost → progressive. Exposure = potential; fines already paid = actual.
9"Which channels does this affect?" / "Link metric to channel."Five channels only: revenues, expenses, assets, liabilities, cost of capital. Margin, turnover, NWC, EVA are not channels.
10"How should this be reflected in a DCF?" / "What does it signal in ROIC?" / "Which scenario type?"Cash flows, discount rate, or growth. Margin → consumer advantage; turnover → production advantage. Target → normative; set of futures → exploratory.

The five factors

Ask the test question for each factor. Tick it only if the case gives evidence. [§3.1, §3.2]

The guide's own worked example: child labor in cocoa (Processed Foods) [§3.2] Direct financial: set aside, because sourcing-program spending was about 1% of revenue, relatively immaterial. Legal: set aside, because companies source rather than farm and enforcement is limited, so they are not subject to those laws. Industry norms: applies (public targets, initiatives, transparency). Stakeholder: applies (lawsuits, certifications, reputation). Innovation: "monitor"; more downside than upside.
Rule for "which N of the five" questionsCount the factors the case evidences. If the stem asks for three, three should have clear support. Do not choose a factor because it is "usually" relevant to the industry.
