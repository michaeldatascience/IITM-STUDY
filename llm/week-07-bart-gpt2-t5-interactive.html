Concept 1

From BERT and GPT to BART

BERT gives bidirectional comprehension; GPT gives autoregressive generation. BART combines an encoder that reads corrupted text bidirectionally with a decoder that reconstructs the original sequence autoregressively.

BERT

Encoder-only, bidirectional self-attention. Predicts masked content using left and right context; strong for comprehension.

GPT

Decoder-only, masked self-attention. Predicts each next token from previous tokens; natural for generation.

BART

Vanilla encoder–decoder Transformer with GELU. Encoder reads corrupted input; decoder predicts the entire clean sequence and receives cross-attention to the encoder.

BART is a denoising autoencoder: corruption creates an input that is incomplete or disordered, while the target remains the original text. Unlike original BERT-style MLM, the decoder predicts a complete output sequence.

GA 7 Q1

Walk through BART’s autoregressive loss

Actual problem

Original target: astronomy is a character building experience [end]. The decoder supplies one probability for each correct next token.

StepCorrect targetProbability−ln p
Average cross-entropy: add the first target token.

The seven correct-token probabilities are .41, .43, .29, .48, .29, .23, .30. The course loss uses their average negative log-likelihood:

L = −(1/7) Σ log p(yt | y<t, corrupted input) ≈ 1.09
Actual GA 7 Q1 — Full answer
The sum of token losses is approximately 7.619. Divide by seven predicted tokens to get 1.088 ≈ 1.09, inside the official accepted range [0.9, 1.1]. Greedy choices do not replace the ground-truth targets in the loss calculation; the decoder remains autoregressive over positions.
Actual December 2025 PYQ Q16 — Doubling a BART encoder sequence

For fixed hidden size, self-attention contains a sequence-pair term proportional to T². Doubling T makes it 4×. The FFN is applied once per position, proportional to T, so it becomes 2×.

Correct statements: self-attention FLOPs increase by factor 4; FFN FLOPs increase by factor 2.

Concept 2

GPT‑2: prompting a single language model

Traditional transfer makes a model copy per task and fine-tunes thousands of supervised examples. Prompting instead places the task in words inside the input.

single-task LM: p(output | input)   →   prompted multi-task view: p(output | input, task)

The task is an instruction prepended or appended to the input. In the lecture example, adding TL;DR: induces summarization. Zero-shot transfer supplies an instruction but no labelled demonstrations; few-shot transfer places a small number of demonstrations in the context.

Why GPT‑2 made this plausible

  • WebText: 40 GB, about 8 million pages, outbound Reddit links with at least 3 karma, excluding Wikipedia.
  • Byte-level BPE vocabulary: 50,257 tokens.
  • Four model sizes: 117M/12 layers up to 1542M/48 layers; largest d_model=1600.
  • Context window 1024; batch size 512; CLM objective.

The 1.5B model transferred zero-shot across language modelling, text8 character modelling, and LAMBADA. It could beat domain-specific language models without their domain training data, yet remained far from fine-tuned SOTA on QA, reading comprehension, summarization and translation. The model still underfit its training set—evidence that more scale could help.

Actual GA 7 Q2 — Which transfer approaches remain appropriate?

Given that the GPT pre-training corpus already contains unlabeled samples from all 12 downstream datasets, the supplied key accepts zero-shot, few-shot, and full fine-tuning.

Interpretation: exposure during CLM pre-training does not prevent any of these adaptation modes. In a real evaluation, one would also examine leakage carefully; the official answer focuses on transfer-method applicability.

Concept 3

T5: make every NLP task text-in, text-out

T5—Text-To-Text Transfer Transformer—uses one encoder–decoder interface and task-specific prefixes, allowing architecture, objective, data, and transfer choices to be compared systematically.

Input

Natural-language text with a task prefix, such as translate English to Tamil:, summarize:, or an STS-B sentence-pair format.

Output

Always text: a translation, summary, similarity score such as 3.8, or classification label such as positive.

Concept 4

The T5 baseline and span-corruption denoising

A common baseline makes comparisons meaningful: 12 encoder and 12 decoder stacks, 220M parameters, C4, and an encoder–decoder denoising objective.

Span corruption with sentinel tokens

Replace each random span—one or more consecutive tokens—with one unique sentinel token in the input. The target concatenates the removed spans, each introduced by its corresponding sentinel, and ends with the next sentinel.

Lecture example

Reveal the three representations

Interactive
What sets this mission apart is the pivotal role of artificial intelligence (AI) in guiding the spacecraft during its critical descent to the moon's surface.
Why this reduces costThe target contains only sentinel markers and removed spans, not a full copy of every unchanged token. The decoder therefore processes a shorter target than full-sequence reconstruction.
Actual GA 7 Q3 — Sentinel target construction

The accepted targets are the options that preserve this structure:

[v] of science is [w] contradictory attitudes [x] counterintuitive they may be [y] how deep truths are winnowed [z]

or the same ordered pattern with sentinel names [a]…[e]. The exact sentinel symbols may differ, but each removed span needs its own ordered marker and the sequence closes with the next sentinel. Official answer: A and B.

Actual GA 7 Q4 — Are sentinel tokens in the vocabulary?
True. The model must encode and generate them; therefore the special sentinel tokens are vocabulary entries.

Training arithmetic

Token-budget calculator

How many pre-training tokens are seen?

Official baseline: B=128, T=512, so B×T=65,536=2¹⁶ tokens per batch. With 2¹⁹ steps, total tokens are 2³⁵≈34B—less than one epoch of 156B-token C4. AdaFactor reduces optimizer memory relative to Adam; the learning-rate schedule is inverse square root with warm-up.

Actual GA 7 Q5 — One million steps and at least 5B tokens

Need 1,000,000 × B × T ≥ 5,000,000,000, hence B×T≥5,000.

  • 8×128=1,024 → 1.024B: no.
  • 64×128=8,192 → 8.192B: yes.
  • 128×512=65,536 → 65.536B: yes.
  • 4×2048=8,192 → 8.192B: yes.

Concept 5

Architecture and unsupervised objectives

The study compares encoder–decoder, decoder-only language models, and prefix-LM while controlling parameters or FLOPs—criteria that cannot always be matched simultaneously.

Prefix language modelling

The input/task prefix attends bidirectionally; the output portion attends autoregressively. This can be implemented with an encoder for input plus decoder for output, or a decoder-only model with a causal-with-prefix mask.

Actual PYQ

For N=10, P=2: prefix block contributes P²=4. Each of 8 output tokens sees both prefix tokens plus its causal output history, giving 3+4+…+10=52. Total = 56 non-infinity entries.

Actual GA 7 Q7 — Parameter count versus compute

A decoder model with P parameters and an encoder–decoder with 2P parameters can have the same computational cost under the T5 comparison. Official answer: True.

The slide’s point is that matching parameter count and matching FLOPs are different comparison criteria. An encoder–decoder with L encoder + L decoder layers has roughly the parameters of a 2L-layer decoder, yet the processing pattern can make compute comparable to an L-layer decoder for the given input/target setup.

Concept 6

Pre-training data: scale is not enough

The lecture separates three effects: corpus scale, corpus quality/domain, and how often examples repeat.

Unfiltered C4

~8× larger

Only language detection filtering. Despite size, performance degrades across all tasks.

RealNews-like

~21× smaller

Filtered to news sources; performs well across many tasks. Quality matters.

Very small alternatives

~42× smaller

WebText-like, Wikipedia, Wikipedia+BookCorpus. In-domain unlabeled data can help matching downstream tasks.

Supported conclusion“More data” is not automatically “better data.” Filtering and domain match can outweigh raw scale. A controlled experiment varies the dataset while holding architecture, objective, training budget and evaluation fixed.

Repetition and memorization

The baseline sees about 2³⁵≈34B tokens once from a large corpus. Shrinking the underlying dataset and repeating it 64 or 256 times creates a small but repeated sample. Performance first degrades slightly, then more strongly; training loss supports memorization.

Concept 7

Fine-tuning strategies

Full fine-tuning updates every parameter for each downstream task. Week 7 studies two cheaper alternatives: adapter layers and gradual unfreezing.

Full fine-tuning

Update all encoder and decoder parameters. Strong baseline, but expensive and requires a full task-specific model copy.

Adapters

Add dense → ReLU → dense after each FFN with a residual connection. Update adapters and layer-normalization parameters only.

Gradual unfreezing

Freeze all layers; unfreeze from the top downward in episodes, simultaneously for corresponding encoder and decoder layers.

Adapter bottleneck dimension d maps d_model → d → d_model. Adapter parameters are typically 0.5–8% of a Transformer layer. Smaller d can suffice for low-resource tasks such as SQuAD in the lecture result; higher-resource tasks need larger d.

Adapter calculator

Estimate trainable adapter weights

Toy calculation

Approximation counts the two dense weight matrices, 2·d_model·d, and ignores biases/layer norm.

For gradual unfreezing with 12 encoder and 12 decoder layers over 2¹⁸ fine-tuning steps, there are 12 episodes; corresponding top layers are unfrozen together every 2¹⁸/12 steps. It speeds training but causes slight performance degradation; schedule tuning may improve it.

Concept 8

Multi-task learning and dataset mixing

T5 can mix labelled and unlabelled datasets because every task uses one text-to-text loss. The hard part is sampling: dataset sizes are extremely unequal.

Examples-Proportional (EP) mixing

rm = min(em,K) / Σn min(en,K)

K caps each dataset’s effective size so C4 cannot dominate without bound. Sample task m with probability r_m.

Temperature mixing

qm = rm1/T / Σ rn1/T

As T increases, probabilities become more uniform; low T makes the distribution more skewed toward large datasets. The lecture reports T=2 works better for some tasks.

Toy sizes (thousands): C4 1000, WMT 400, SQuAD 100, GLUE 50. The mechanism—not these toy numbers—is the course concept.

Findings and variants

  • There is a sweet spot for K; simply raising or lowering it does not monotonically help.
  • General multi-task training performs worse than pre-train-then-fine-tune overall.
  • Multi-task pre-training followed by fine-tuning becomes comparable to the baseline.
  • Leave-one-out pre-training is only slightly worse, suggesting transfer to a task unseen during pre-training.
  • Supervised-only multi-task pre-training helps mainly on translation; large unsupervised pre-training + fine-tuning still dominates overall.

Concept 9

Using more compute and pushing T5’s limits

With 4× compute, one may train longer, increase batch size, enlarge the model, or ensemble independent models. Each spends compute differently.

Compute-budget explorer

Choose how to spend 4× compute

Longer training or a larger batch consistently improves the baseline. Scaling parameters—even while reducing training steps—wins on many tasks. Ensembling is reasonable across tasks and especially useful for translation. There is no universal winner: larger models cost more during fine-tuning and inference.

Final T5 design selected after the study

Scaling parameters improves all reported task groups except translation, and the study achieves SOTA on multiple benchmarks.

Week 7 cheat sheet

Models

BART = corrupted input → bidirectional encoder → autoregressive decoder → complete clean target. GPT‑2 expresses the task as prompt text and demonstrates zero-shot transfer. T5 unifies every task as text-in/text-out with a task prefix.

T5 baseline

12 encoder + 12 decoder stacks, 220M parameters, C4, span-corruption denoising. B=128, T=512, 2¹⁹ steps → 2³⁵≈34B tokens.

Sentinel rule

One sentinel replaces one consecutive corrupted span. Target: sentinel₁ + removed span₁ + sentinel₂ + removed span₂ + … + final next sentinel. Sentinels are in the vocabulary.

Architecture/objective

Prefix LM: prefix bidirectional, output causal. Encoder–decoder wins for fixed parameters/FLOPs; decoder-only prefix LM can be similar. BERT-style objective best overall; deshuffling worse; span corruption strong. Rate has little effect; span length 3 strong for non-translation.

Data

Quality and domain matter, not scale alone. Unfiltered C4 larger but worse; RealNews-like smaller but strong. Heavy repetition produces degradation and memorization.

Transfer

Adapters update small dense–ReLU–dense modules plus layer norm. Gradual unfreezing proceeds top-down and speeds training with slight degradation. T5 classification targets are strings; no task-specific classification head is necessary.

Multi-task/scaling

EP mixing caps size with K. Temperature T↑ makes sampling more uniform. Pre-train then fine-tune generally beats plain joint multi-task training. More parameters and more tokens help, but larger models increase fine-tuning/inference cost.

Exam numbers

  • GA BART loss ≈ 1.09.
  • One million steps: require B×T≥5000 to see ≥5B tokens.
  • Prefix mask N=10,P=2: 56 allowed entries.
  • Doubling sequence length: attention 4×, FFN 2×.
  • T5 final: 1M steps × 2048 × 512 ≈ 1T tokens.
